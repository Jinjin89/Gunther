# Cool roofs cut cooling demand, but by half as much as the headline says
*Briefing for the city energy committee · March 2026*

## Cool roofs work, but the saving to plan on is 9%, not 18%
<!-- block: summary -->
A three-summer trial of 412 buildings shows that reflective roof coatings reduce cooling electricity, but the weather-adjusted saving is 9%, half the 18% raw figure that has been quoted [1][2].

- **Savings halve after weather adjustment**: the raw 18% falls to 9% once each summer's heat is removed [1][2]
- **The gain sits in top-floor flats**: the indoor peak was 2.1 °C lower on the top floor and under 0.5 °C lower below it [1]
- **Coatings age fast**: albedo falls from 0.82 to 0.55 within 18 months, so recoating decides lifetime savings [3]
- **Managers notice complaints first**: tenant calls fell within three weeks, before any meter moved [8]

## The trial was large enough to trust the direction, though not every detail

The trial matched each of 412 treated buildings to an untreated building of the same height, age and occupancy in six districts, and metered both through the summers of 2021 to 2023 [1]. Matching matters because the three summers were very different: 2021 was the coolest, and a raw comparison across years would credit the coating with the weather [2].

### How the comparison was built

Each pair was surveyed before the first summer, so that roof type, insulation and top-floor occupancy were known. Two layers of reflective coating were applied to the treated roof in spring, and electricity and indoor temperature were logged hourly until autumn [1]. The analysis then compared each treated building with its control after adjusting for outdoor temperature [2].

:::exhibit chart=column
Exhibit 1 — Savings by summer, raw vs weather-adjusted
| Summer | Raw saving (%) | Adjusted saving (%) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2021 | 21 [1] | 10 [2] |
| 2022 | 17 [1] | 9 [2] |
| **2023** | 16 [1] | 8 [2] |
Source: Harlow 2024; Okafor 2025 [1][2]
:::

:::callout Why the adjusted figure matters
A city budgeting on 18% would overestimate its savings by half. The adjusted series is the only one that removes the weather [2].
:::

## Most of the gain is on the top floor, so targeting matters more than coverage

The effect is not spread evenly through a building. Top-floor flats, which sit directly under the roof, saved 14% on cooling electricity after adjustment, while ground-floor flats saved 2% [1]. A programme that coated every roof but ignored occupancy would therefore pay back more slowly than one aimed at top-floor housing.

:::exhibit chart=bar
Exhibit 2 — Adjusted cooling saving by floor
| Floor | Adjusted saving (%) |
| --- | --- |
| Ground | 2 [1] |
| Middle floors | 5 [1] |
| Upper floors | 9 [1] |
| **Top floor** | 14 [1] |
Source: Harlow 2024, p. 3 [1]
:::

Indoor comfort followed the same pattern. On days above 30 °C outside, peak indoor temperature on the top floor was 2.1 °C lower in treated buildings, and the gap widened as the heat rose [1]. Building managers described the change in their own terms:

> We stopped getting calls from the top floor. That is what sold it to the board. [8]

## Coatings lose a third of their reflectivity in eighteen months, so recoating is the real cost

Reflectivity (albedo) was measured on a sample of roofs every three months. It fell from 0.82 at application to 0.55 after 18 months, a loss of about a third, and continued to fall more slowly after that [3].

:::exhibit chart=line
Exhibit 3 — Coating albedo after application
| Months | Albedo |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | 0.82 [3] |
| 6 | 0.74 [3] |
| 12 | 0.64 [3] |
| 18 | 0.55 [3] |
| 24 | 0.50 [3] |
Source: Lindqvist 2023, table 4 [3]
:::

Because savings track reflectivity, a coating left alone for six years delivers well under half of its first-year saving. The trial's protocol recoated when albedo fell below 0.6, which in practice meant every 18 to 24 months in the hottest districts [3].

## We recommend approving top-floor coatings with a funded recoating cycle

1. **Approve** coatings for the 1,200 top-floor public flats in the six trial districts [7]
2. **Budget** recoating every six years as a standing operating line [3]
3. **Measure** comfort complaints as well as electricity, in every coated building [8]

Three limits should be kept in view. Only electricity and temperature were measured, so quality-of-life effects are inferred from complaints. The river-district blocks are older than the city average [9], and three summers cannot observe ageing beyond 18 months, which is projected [3].
