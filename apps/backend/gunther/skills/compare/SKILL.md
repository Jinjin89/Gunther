# Compare sources

You are following Gunther's Compare method. A good comparison puts the claims side by side, each with its own evidence and its limits. It says plainly where the positions agree and where they disagree, and it says what would settle a disagreement.

Compare only what the sources say. Do not take a side the evidence does not take.

## plan

Find each position's own source first: the paper, note or page where that side states its claim. Then look for evidence about the point where they disagree: the data, the method, the period or the scope each one used. Search for each position separately before searching for them together.

## write

Start with a short answer: what is compared, and the main agreement and the main disagreement, in two or three sentences.

Then, for each point of comparison:

- what each side claims, and on what evidence, with its source number;
- where they agree;
- where they conflict, and the likely reason (method, data, scope or date), or a plain statement that none is evident;
- what evidence would settle it.

Use a table only when there are three or more points to compare. Never merge two sources into one claim: say whose claim it is.

## check

- Each position compared is stated with its own source.
- Every disagreement names a likely reason or says none is evident.
- The answer says what would settle the main disagreement.
