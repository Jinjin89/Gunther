# Glossary of urban heat terms

This glossary explains the terms used in the notes in this library. Each entry gives a short definition and, where it helps, a sentence on why the term matters for roofs and cities.

## Albedo

Albedo is the share of incoming sunlight that a surface reflects, expressed as a number between 0 and 1. A value of 0 means that the surface absorbs all the sunlight that reaches it, and a value of 1 means that it reflects all of it. Dark roofs have low albedo and bright roofs have high albedo. Albedo is measured over the whole solar spectrum, visible light included, and it changes as a surface ages and collects dirt.

## Urban heat island

An urban heat island is the tendency of a built-up area to be warmer than the countryside around it, especially at night. It comes from dark surfaces that store heat, from the lack of vegetation that would cool the air by evaporation, from the shape of streets that trap heat, and from heat released by traffic, industry and air conditioning. The difference can be several degrees in a large city on a calm summer night.

## Cool roof

A cool roof is a roof that is designed to reflect more sunlight and absorb less heat than a conventional roof. It can be made by applying a reflective coating, by covering the roof with light-coloured sheets or tiles, or by choosing a bright material from the start. A cool roof stays cooler than a dark roof in sunshine, which reduces the heat passed into the building below.

## Green roof

A green roof is a roof covered with a layer of soil or growing medium and planted with vegetation. It cools the building by shading the roof and by evaporation from the plants and soil, and it also holds rainwater and can provide habitat for insects and birds. It is heavier than other roofs and needs more care.

## Solar reflectance index (SRI)

The solar reflectance index, or SRI, is a 0–100 scale combining reflectance and emittance, where a standard black surface is 0 and a standard white surface is 100. Reflectance describes how much sunlight a surface sends back, and emittance describes how well it gives off the heat it has absorbed. The index puts the two together into a single number, so that roofing products can be compared without having to look at both properties. Some products can score slightly above 100 or below 0 if they are better or worse than the standard surfaces.

## Cooling degree days

Cooling degree days count how hot a period has been, as the sum over its days of the amount by which the daily mean temperature exceeds a base temperature at which buildings need no cooling. A summer with more cooling degree days is hotter and calls for more cooling energy.
