# Velmora Climate Plan 2026

## Summary

The Velmora Climate Plan 2026 sets out the city's measures for adapting to a hotter climate and for cutting the emissions of its buildings over the next decade. It was adopted by the city council after a public consultation and replaces the adaptation strategy of 2018. The plan has four parts: public space and trees, water and flooding, the energy use of buildings, and support for residents in vulnerable situations. This extract covers the part on buildings and, within it, the measure on reflective roofs.

## Why roofs

Most of the city's housing stock was built before cooling was a consideration, and many apartment blocks have flat roofs in dark bitumen or tar. Heat in the top-floor flats of such blocks is the single most common complaint that reaches the city's housing advice service in summer. The plan treats a reflective roof coating as the cheapest measure that is both quick to apply and visible in its effect, and it uses the results of the city's own trial as the basis for the target. The plan cites the 18% cooling-energy saving from Harlow 2024 as the basis for the target, and treats it as the expected reduction in electricity use for cooling in the buildings that are coated.

## Target

The plan sets a target of 30% of residential roofs coated by 2030. Progress will be reported every year in the city's climate dashboard, counting roofs rather than floor area, and the target will be reviewed in 2028. Roofs on listed buildings and roofs that are unsuitable for coating, because of their structure or because they carry solar panels over most of their area, are excluded from the count of eligible roofs.

## Subsidy

To make the target reachable, the city will pay a subsidy of €14 per square metre of coated roof. The subsidy is paid to the owner after an inspection confirms that the coating has been applied to the specification in the plan, and it can be combined with the national renovation grant but not with other municipal grants for the same roof. Housing associations that coat more than fifty roofs in a year may apply for a single combined payment, which reduces the paperwork on both sides.

## Monitoring and review

The environment office will keep a sample of coated roofs under observation, measuring their reflectance each spring and comparing the electricity use of coated buildings with that of similar uncoated ones. The results will be published together with the annual progress report. If the observed savings turn out to be far from the figure assumed in the plan, the council will decide in 2028 whether to adjust the target, the subsidy or both.

## Other measures in the buildings chapter

The chapter also covers shading requirements for new windows facing south and west, a register of buildings with the highest summer risk for older residents, and guidance for owners on night ventilation. These measures are described in their own sections and are not repeated here.
