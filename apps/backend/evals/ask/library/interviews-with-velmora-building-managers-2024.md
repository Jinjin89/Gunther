# Interviews with Velmora building managers (2024)

## About the interviews

In the autumn of 2024 the city's housing advice service interviewed eleven managers of apartment buildings whose roofs had been coated with a reflective finish. The managers work for housing associations and for private landlords; they were asked what had changed in the buildings since the coating, what problems they had dealt with, and what they would advise others to do. The interviews lasted between twenty and forty minutes and were recorded with permission. What follows is a summary organised by theme, not a transcript, and the numbers of managers mentioning each point are given where they are meaningful.

## Glare

The most frequent complaint was about glare. Residents of taller neighbouring buildings said that sunlight reflected from the coated roofs shone into their windows in the late afternoon, especially in spring and autumn when the sun is lower. Six of the eleven managers had received at least one complaint of this kind, and two of them had been asked by the neighbours' association to do something about it. The options discussed were a slightly less reflective finish on roofs that are overlooked, low parapets, and the planting of climbers or screens on neighbouring facades. No manager had found a fully satisfactory solution, and several asked that planners consider the height of surrounding buildings before choosing which roofs to coat.

## Maintenance

Managers agreed that the coatings need cleaning every 2 years to keep their reflectance. Without cleaning, roofs gradually became greyer, and the managers who had cleaned theirs said that a wash with water and a soft brush, done by two people in a morning, restored most of the original brightness. The cost of cleaning was small compared with the cost of the coating itself, but it was a new task that did not fit easily into the maintenance contracts the buildings already had. Three managers said that they had added it to the annual roof inspection, and one had trained the caretaker to do it. Others admitted that the cleaning had been forgotten in the first year.

## Tenants' experience

Opinions about comfort were positive but impressionistic. One manager reported that tenants in top-floor flats said the flats felt cooler, and that fewer of them had asked about installing air conditioning. The manager did not have measurements to back this up and stressed that it was what tenants said, not a result. Others said that they had received no comments in either direction, which they took to mean that nothing had got worse.

## Advice from managers

The managers' advice to colleagues was practical. Check the condition of the roof first, because coating a roof that leaks only hides the problem. Tell tenants in advance what is being done and why. Agree on who is responsible for cleaning before the work begins. Ask neighbours about glare early, when a change of finish is still cheap. Several said they would coat further roofs, provided the cleaning arrangement was settled.
