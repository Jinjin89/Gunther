# Green roofs versus cool roofs: a review (Lindqvist 2023)

## Scope

This review compares two ways of treating a building's roof to reduce summer heat: planting it, which gives a green roof, and covering it with a reflective surface, which gives a cool roof. It draws on thirty-eight published field studies and modelling papers from northern and central Europe, North America and East Asia. It does not discuss any single city's programme; the studies it reviews are those that report measurements on real buildings.

## Heat reduction

Green roofs lower the peak roof surface temperature by 15–20 °C compared with a conventional dark roof. They do so partly by shading the roof membrane, partly through evaporation from the soil and leaves. The effect depends on the thickness of the growing medium, the choice of plants and how well the roof is watered in dry spells. A neglected green roof in a dry summer performs much worse than the studies of well-kept roofs suggest.

Cool roofs reduce surface temperatures through reflection alone. Their effect is more predictable, since it depends mainly on the reflectance of the surface, and it starts the day the coating is applied. Over time reflectance declines as dirt collects, which makes maintenance an important part of the comparison.

## Cost

The costs differ greatly. Green roofs cost 4 to 6 times more than cool roofs per square metre once the structural reinforcement, the waterproofing layer, the growing medium and the planting are included. Their annual maintenance, mostly weeding, watering and checking drains, is also higher. A cool roof coating, by contrast, can be applied by a small crew in a day or two to a roof that is already sound.

## Rainwater

Green roofs retain 40–60% of rainfall, which delays and reduces runoff in heavy storms. For cities with combined sewers or with a record of local flooding this is a major benefit, and it is the main reason why green roofs are favoured in stormwater plans. Cool roofs have no meaningful effect on rainfall runoff.

## Energy evidence

Most of the energy evidence for green roofs is from a few well-instrumented buildings. In Stockholm office buildings, green roofs cut cooling energy by 18%. The review warns that this is a result for one building type in one climate, with large glazed areas and mechanical cooling, and that it cannot be carried over to flats in other cities without care. Evidence on cool roofs comes from a wider range of buildings and climates but is also uneven in quality.

## Conclusions

The authors conclude that cool roofs give more heat reduction per euro, because their cost is so much lower and their cooling effect, while smaller at the roof surface, is of the same order for the rooms below. Green roofs win on stormwater and biodiversity: they hold water, support insects and birds, and make a roof pleasant to look at and sometimes to use. The authors recommend that cities decide which of these goals matters most for each district and do not treat the two kinds of roof as substitutes for each other.
